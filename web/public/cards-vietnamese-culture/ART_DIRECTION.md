# Vietnamese Culture Day Tarot Art Direction

This deck is for Vietnamese Culture Day, 24/11. It should not read as generic
Vietnamese decoration. It should be built around the meanings specific to this
day and let the full 78-card deck cover the cultural details over time.

## Core Principle

Do not force every Vietnamese cultural detail into one card.

Each card should use only the details that fit its tarot archetype. The whole
deck, not a single image, is responsible for covering Vietnamese cultural
memory, craft, architecture, landscape, material life, performance, and
transmission.

## Four Main Axes

Use these as the strongest recurring ideas because they belong to the day, not
just to general Vietnamese culture.

### Guiding Light

Inspired by the idea: "Văn hóa soi đường cho quốc dân đi".

Useful symbols:

- lanterns
- torches
- oil lamps
- beams of light falling onto a road
- a lit path, threshold, or doorway

This is especially tarot-friendly and can appear across many cards as a visual
language of guidance, awakening, and orientation.

### Dong Son Bronze Drum Sun

Use the Dong Son bronze drum sun as the strongest solar/cosmic motif.

Useful symbols:

- circular sun geometry
- radiating rays
- Lac bird rhythm around a disc
- bronze texture and stamped pattern

This motif is strong enough to become the card back. Use it carefully on faces
so it stays meaningful rather than becoming a repeated logo.

### Hanoi Opera House

The Hanoi Opera House matters because it hosted the opening of the 1946
National Culture Conference.

Use it as a background or architectural anchor for a few formal, civic, or
ceremonial cards. Do not use it on every card.

### Transmission

The official message of the day centers on people creating, preserving, and
passing culture on.

Useful symbols:

- an older hand passing an object to a younger hand
- a craft tool being taught
- a book, lamp, brush, bowl, seed, coin, or instrument transferred between
  generations
- shared work at a table, loom, kiln, or stage

## Suit Replacements

Use these suit systems for the Vietnamese Culture Day deck.

| RWS suit | Vietnamese suit | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wands | Bamboo | Bamboo poles, tam vong staffs, shoots, fishing lift poles; will, growth, resilience |
| Cups | Ceramics | Chu Dau-style bowls, teapots, water ladles; hospitality, feeling, relationship |
| Swords | Brush and Ink | Brushes, inkstones, Nom bamboo slips; intellect and language without war imagery |
| Pentacles | Rice and Old Coins | Rice bundles, square-holed coins on cord; material life, craft, harvest, livelihood |

Prefer **Brush and Ink** for the Swords suit. Musical instruments can support
some cards, but brush and ink better fit culture, writing, thought, and
transmission.

## Visual Language

### Color

Prefer:

- earth brown
- son red / brick red
- turmeric yellow
- blue-and-white ceramic glaze
- deep indigo
- muted jade or bamboo green
- paper-do ivory

Avoid bright red and bright yellow as the main palette because they can make the
deck feel like a propaganda poster.

### Materials

Build the look from Vietnamese material culture:

- Dong Ho / Kim Hoang woodblock print energy
- paper-do fiber texture
- soft ink bleeding
- ceramic glaze
- carved wood
- bamboo
- bronze
- woven mat texture
- lacquer and mother-of-pearl, only where the card calls for it

### Borders

Use one of these border languages:

- Dong Son bronze drum pattern running around the card
- village communal-house lattice / window-bar pattern
- restrained folk-print geometric ornament
- four circular corner medallions with a yellow star or Dong Son sun-rosette on
  a son-red ground

Borders should unify the deck but may vary enough to avoid sameness.
Treat the corner star as an ornamental tarot medallion, not as a flag or
political emblem.

### Backgrounds

Prefer Vietnamese spaces and landscapes over European castles:

- communal house
- pagoda
- river
- rice field
- village path
- craft workshop
- kiln
- stage or theater curtain
- Hanoi Opera House for a small number of formal cards

## Avoid

Do not use:

- national leaders
- national flags
- national emblem
- political iconography
- propaganda poster composition

The day has a political history, but turning political symbols into divination
cards can create unnecessary backlash.

Do not place Vietnamese religions or sacred performance forms on dark cards in
a way that stigmatizes them. In particular, avoid using hau dong, Mother
Goddess worship, or tuong masks for **XV The Devil** or **XIII Death**.

For difficult cards, prefer neutral metaphors:

- **XV The Devil**: bamboo cords or bindings around feet, dependency, bargain,
  habit, constraint.
- **XIII Death**: post-harvest field, cut rice stalks, stubble, empty land,
  ending before the next season.

Avoid cramming lotus imagery into the deck. Lotus can appear where it truly fits,
but it must not become the default symbol for every Vietnamese card.

## Text And Format

- Final card assets are `600x900` WebP.
- Generate full vertical 2:3 card faces.
- Leave a blank top area for Roman numeral or rank overlay.
- Leave a blank bottom title band for English title overlay.
- Generate art without readable text, numbers, letters, pseudo-writing, logos,
  or watermarks.
